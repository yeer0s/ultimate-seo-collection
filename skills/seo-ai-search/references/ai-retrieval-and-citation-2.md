## Answer block — put this near the top of every content page

```
Quick answer:
[2-4 sentences directly answering the page's primary question with specific facts.]

[Optional: 1-2 line caveat or qualifier — "depends on usage" / "varies by region" / "requires X".]
```

Example:
> **Quick answer:** The best electricity provider in Portugal depends on your usage profile, contracted power, tariff cycle, payment method, and whether you want fixed or indexed pricing. For [usage band, e.g. ≤ N kWh/month] at [contracted power] on [tariff cycle], [provider A] and [provider B] had the lowest total monthly cost on [tariff date] ([source], offer conditions: [conditions]). Compare the standing charge alongside kWh — many "cheap" tariffs hide cost there.

Every bracket is a placeholder: fill it only from dated, verified tariff data (source, date, contracted power, offer conditions), or drop the sentence. Never ship provider names or a usage threshold from memory.

This block is a candidate for AI Overviews and featured snippets to lift `[hypothesis]`; no engine documents that it will be lifted, and Google lists no technical requirement for AI features beyond indexation and snippet eligibility `[documented: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features, 2026-10-11]`.

## AI-friendly content structure

Order matters. Use this skeleton for content pages targeting AI citation:

1. **Definition / direct answer** (2-4 sentences)
2. **Quick comparison or recommendation** (table or 3-bullet list)
3. **Step-by-step process** (if relevant)
4. **Pros and cons** (matched lists)
5. **Common mistakes / pitfalls**
6. **FAQ** (real questions, real answers)
7. **Sources / methodology**

Each section is a candidate for being lifted independently.

## Entity SEO

Modern SEO is about entities, not just keywords. Help engines understand who/what your brand, products, and people *are*:

- **Schema.org `Organization`** with `sameAs` to LinkedIn, Wikipedia, Crunchbase, official social.
- **Schema.org `Person`** for authors with `knowsAbout` and `alumniOf`.
- **Wikidata entry** for brand and key people if eligible.
- **Consistent NAP** (Name, Address, Phone) across the web for local entities.
- **Structured About page** with company info, leadership, history.
- **Press mentions** with backlinks from authoritative outlets.
- **Knowledge panel claim** in Google Search if eligible.

Clear entity definition may make citation and attribution more likely `[hypothesis]`; no engine documents it as a requirement for being cited.

## Conversion SEO

SEO without conversion is vanity. Every page needs a job that ladders to revenue.

### Conversion goals by page type

| Page type | Conversion goal |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Blog / guide | Email capture, calculator use, move to comparison |
| Comparison page | Lead form / quote request |
| Versus page | Lead form / signup |
| Local page | Call / contact / quote |
| Product page | Purchase / add to cart |
| Service page | Quote request / consultation booking |
| Tool / calculator | Account creation / email capture / save results |
| FAQ page | Move user to money page |
| Case study | Inquiry / book demo |
| Methodology page | Build trust → return to comparison |

### CTA inventory

Match CTA to intent:

| User state | CTA |
|------------|-----|
| Just learning | "Read our guide" / email signup |
| Comparing options | "Compare providers" / "Calculate savings" |
| Ready to switch | "Get a free analysis" / "Switch online" |
| Has objection | "Check eligibility" / "See methodology" |
| Has data | "Upload your bill" / "Run my numbers" |
| Wants confidence | "Read reviews" / "See case studies" |
| Local | "Book consultation" / "Get local quote" |
| Decision-deferred | "Set price alert" / "Save my comparison" |

A page with the wrong CTA for the user's stage will fail to convert even with great traffic.

### CRO checklist for SEO landing pages

- [ ] **Above-the-fold value proposition** — user understands the benefit in 2 seconds
- [ ] **Quick answer block** — for users who scan
- [ ] **Trust badges** — regulator approvals, partner logos, certifications, review aggregates
- [ ] **Comparison table or decision aid** — helps users decide on the page
- [ ] **Sticky CTA on long pages** — never make the user scroll back
- [ ] **FAQ that handles objections** — pricing, safety, switching cost, hidden fees
- [ ] **Real reviews / testimonials** — with names, photos, specific outcomes
- [ ] **Methodology link** — for trust-sensitive verticals (financial, health)
- [ ] **Fast form** — minimum fields; progressive profiling for the rest
- [ ] **Progress indicator** — on multi-step forms
- [ ] **Privacy reassurance** — especially for data-heavy lead-gen
- [ ] **Mobile-first form design** — most SEO traffic is mobile
- [ ] **Error states that recover** — no dead-end forms
- [ ] **Confirmation page** that converts further (next step, email captured, etc.)

## E-E-A-T trust signals (engineered, not claimed)

| Signal | Implementation |
|--------|----------------|
| Real author | Name, bio, credentials, headshot, link to LinkedIn or pubs |
| "Reviewed by" | Editorial process — show who validated the content |
| Sources | Inline links to regulators, government data, research |
| Last updated | Visible date; updated within reasonable cadence for the topic |
| Company info | Address, contact, legal entity, registration number |
| About page | Real story, team, mission |
| Privacy policy | Especially for lead-gen / data-heavy sites |
| Terms | Clear user expectations |
| Multiple contact options | Email, phone, form, chat |
| Reviews / testimonials | Genuine, verifiable, ideally with case study links |
| Corrections policy | For editorial content — shows you care about accuracy |
| Methodology | How comparisons / rankings / pricing are calculated |
| Disclosures | Affiliate / lead-gen / sponsorship relationships |

For YMYL (Your Money or Your Life) niches — finance, health, legal — these signals are held to matter more `[hypothesis]`. A named reviewer is a trust signal for readers; that a reviewer badge outranks a longer article is unsupported — do not promise a ranking effect from it `[hypothesis]`.

## Methodology page (critical for comparison/intermediation platforms)

Required sections:
- **How we compare providers** — what data we collect, from where, how often
- **How we make money** — partnerships, lead-gen fees, affiliate commissions; full disclosure
- **How we rank offers** — what factors weight the order, are there sponsored placements (and how marked)
- **How we verify prices** — source, refresh cadence, error correction
- **How often we update data** — by data type
- **What's included and excluded** — coverage limitations
- **How users can report incorrect information** — feedback mechanism

This page is plausibly one of the strongest trust signals you can engineer `[hypothesis]`. Link to it from every comparison page `[unvalidated house heuristic]`.

## Voice and AI Overview optimization tactics

Drafting habits `[unvalidated house heuristic]`, not engine requirements:

- **Conversational H2s** — "How do I switch electricity provider in Portugal?" beats "Switching electricity providers."
- **Direct answers immediately under H2s** — don't bury the lede.
- **Tables for comparison data** — voice assistants and AI engines parse tables cleanly.
- **Specific numbers over vague language** — "saves 12-18% on average" beats "saves money."
- **Cited primary sources** — "according to ERSE's 2026 Q1 report..." builds AI trust.
- **Question-and-answer FAQ blocks** with `FAQPage` schema where genuine.
- **Featured-snippet-shaped paragraphs** — 40-60 words, self-contained, factual.

## Tracking AI visibility

Tools that monitor AI/LLM mentions of brands:
- **Brand monitoring** — track mentions in AI answers (manual + tools like Profound, Otterly, Peec AI)
- **LLM citation tracking** — see if your brand/site appears in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot answers for target queries
- **Entity audit** — verify Wikidata + knowledge graph consistency
- **SERP feature trackers** — track snippets, AI Overviews, PAA presence per query

Two engine-side reports exist: **Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance** (public preview, 2026-02-10) shows "how often your content is cited in generative answers" across Microsoft Copilot and AI-generated summaries in Bing `[documented: https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview, 2026-10-11]`, and **Google Search Console's generative AI performance report** "shows data about how your site performs in generative AI features on Google Search" `[documented: https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139, 2026-10-11]`. Neither covers ChatGPT, Perplexity or Claude, so manual checks of target queries remain the complement: build a checklist of 20-50 commercial queries and check monthly.

## Key risks in the AI search era

- **Zero-click acceleration** — AI Overviews answer many queries without sending traffic. Optimize for citation + brand awareness, not just clicks.
  When clicks fall while rankings hold, triage it as a possible AI-Overview CTR loss before treating it as a penalty: skill `seo-technical` › incident-triage.
- **Hallucinated facts** — AI engines can mis-cite your content. Make claims unambiguous and easy to extract.
- **Source aggregation** — AI engines summarize multiple sources; your unique angle is what gets cited.
- **Brand confusion** — without strong entity definition, AI engines may attribute your work to competitors.

## When this stops mattering

If your SERP shows no AI Overviews, no featured snippets, and no PAA — you're in a "blue links only" niche (rare in 2026). Optimize classical ranking signals first, AI optimization second.

If your SERP is dominated by AI surfaces — AI optimization is more important than ranking #1, because #1 with no AI citation may get less traffic than #5 with AI citation.

Check the SERP. Optimize for what's there.
