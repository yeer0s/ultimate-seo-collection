## AVOID — the evidence argues against

Nine entries. Eight of them rest on a single sample from one publisher (seven printed in the experimental part, #67 in the academic record) with the remedy written as "what to stop"; only #173 has a full entry. An AVOID mark on one sample is a well-designed warning, not a law — but the burden of proof sits on whoever wants to do the thing anyway.

- **#173 Target high volume keyword variants in title tags −20% organic sessions.** 4 samples, 2 controlled (moz.com 2021 A/B −20%, searchpilot.com 2025 A/B −11%); a before-after with no control on 200 URLs showed +9%, so the values cross zero and no median is printed (reported −27% to +9%). Bundle: meta descriptions changed with the titles. Study caveat: the finding relies on a single page test where meta information was changed alongside titles without isolated controls. Remedy printed: revert titles and metas to their original specific wording on a test set against a control; keep the original patterns if sessions recover over 28 days. Not a stuffing case — the failure is intent drift toward broad terms.
- **#157 Add product prices to title tags −15% organic sessions.** 1 sample, 2023. Remedy: remove the static price token from the e-commerce title template, restoring product name and brand. Read against #155 (MAINSTREAM, dynamic *lowest* price +12%): the two treatments differ and neither transfers to the other.
- **#158 Append static rental prices to location title tags −7% organic sessions.** 1 sample, 2025–2026. Remedy: drop the static price string from location titles.
- **#165 Remove synonym keywords from listing title tags −27% organic sessions.** 1 sample, one source with 4 values, reported −27% to −20%, 2025. Remedy: restore the full set of secondary terms and synonyms; do not strip descriptive variations across listing pages. Removing synonyms is not the same act as removing stuffing.
- **#127 Swap numbers for emoji digits in meta descriptions −5% organic sessions.** 1 sample, one source with 4 values, reported −9% to −4%, 2023. Remedy: replace unicode number symbols with plain digits. A different intervention from #156 (emojis in titles, IT DEPENDS).
- **#98 Remove self-referential breadcrumb links −5.5% organic clicks.** 1 sample, 2025. Remedy: restore the active anchor on the final breadcrumb item across listing templates.
- **#123 Remove multi-location map components −7% organic sessions.** 1 sample, 2025. Remedy: restore the embedded map widget below the address block on location pages.
- **#179 Move the search widget above the hero image −7% organic sessions.** 1 sample, 2023. Remedy: move it back below the hero or primary heading so readable intro content precedes interactive tools.
- **#67 Add keyword-stuffed passages to web page content −10% impressions.** 1 sample, 2024; study caveat: benchmark metric, not a live search console. Remedy: remove forced keyword lists and repetitive phrasing added for AI tools. The one AVOID that coincides with an existing hard refusal (keyword stuffing, skill `seo-optimizer` § Hard refusals).

## IT DEPENDS — the condition decides

Six entries where published sources measure opposite directions and the context is named. The study's own framing: of the twelve sides across these 6 entries, 2 rest on at least one controlled test and the rest do not; on 1 of the 6 the sources do not settle which single variable decides. There is no pooled figure for any of them — "averaging the two sides would describe neither." Read each as a question to answer on your own site.

- **#13 Submit disavowal files for identified spam links** (E 38.3 / A 53.3, 3 samples, bundle with outreach and reconsideration requests). Sides: *direction only* for sites disavowing proactively without a manual penalty (0 controlled tests); *+80% organic sessions* for sites recovering from an active manual link penalty (0 controlled tests). Moderator: "The presence of an active manual penalty from search engines." Ask first: "Does your website currently have an active manual link penalty?"
- **#21 Add AI-generated content blocks** (E 75.1 / A 53.6, 6 samples). Sides: *+73% citation rate* for product or advertiser pages enriched with targeted AI copy (1 controlled test); *−35% organic sessions* for entire websites published heavily with mass-generated text (0 controlled tests). Moderator: "Targeted page enhancement versus aggressive sitewide publishing." Ask first: "Are you enriching specific pages or publishing AI copy sitewide?"
- **#53 Expand collection pages and publish search guides** (E 41.7 / A 52.8, 3 samples, bundle with schema). Sides: *+18% impressions* for service sites combining technical debt fixes with targeted content (0 controlled tests); *−22% organic sessions* for e-commerce stores expanding collection pages and shopping guides (0 controlled tests). Moderator: "Whether the site is e-commerce or service-based with tech fixes." Ask first: "Are you modifying e-commerce collection pages or repairing technical crawl issues?" Note the study's recipe here is a *rollback* test: remove the guides on a test group and see whether sessions recover.
- **#125 Set primary business category to specific cuisine** (E 35.0 / A 58.2, 2 samples). Sides: *−30 positions* for a shift from a specific service category to a broader label (0 controlled tests); *+3.4 positions* for a shift from a generic to a specific niche category (0 controlled tests). Moderator: "Whether the category change increases or decreases specificity." Ask first: "Is your proposed category more specific than your current primary category?"
- **#156 Add emojis to title tags** (E 60.0 / A 55.8, 3 samples). Sides: *−6% organic sessions* for thematic content pages testing emojis in isolation (1 controlled test, searchpilot.com 2023); *+1,851 impressions* for new or redesigned pages launched with multiple SEO improvements (0 controlled tests). Moderator: "Unclear, as the positive test bundled emojis with other site updates." Ask first: "Are you adding emojis alone or alongside major content and structural overhauls?" This is the one entry where the deciding variable is not settled — and the only controlled side is negative.
- **#174 Include target keywords in page URLs** (E 35.5 / A 58.6, 2 samples). Sides: *−38% organic sessions* for pages changing web addresses to target broad high-volume terms (0 controlled tests, moz.com 2021, 200 URLs); *direction only* for specific service and skills pages adding targeted role terms (0 controlled tests). Moderator: "The specificity of the keywords added to the page web addresses." Ask first: "Are you adding specific role modifiers or broad high-volume keywords to your page URLs?" The study's caveat adds loss of equity during redirect processing as an unexcluded cause.

## What the industry has NOT tested

- **Coverage is lopsided.** Content depth has 36 entries, title tags 20, internal linking 18, local SEO 15, technical infrastructure 11 — against 10 for AI visibility (GEO) and 7 for answer formatting. The study's line: the industry tests content, titles and internal links; almost nobody tests GEO. The two most-talked-about topics have the thinnest evidence, so any GEO or answer-format recommendation carries EXPERIMENTAL-grade confidence language even when the mark says MAINSTREAM (three of the seven answer-format entries are EXPERIMENTAL; of the ten GEO entries two are ACADEMIC, one is AVOID on a benchmark metric, and both llms.txt entries carry "mechanism unconfirmed").
- **The corpus sees only the open web.** The best work lives in LinkedIn posts, private communities, Slack and Discord groups, conference decks and NDA'd client work; nothing was taken from behind a paywall, login or form. The areas practitioners talk about rather than publish are the most understated here.
- **Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.** The study does not claim that tactics missing from it do not work — only that nobody published a measurement meeting its criteria. Its catalogue is capped at 100 rows; 40 further tactics cleared the same entry condition and are held for the next edition. 61 entries met the shortlist condition; 17 are printed.
- **It is not a meta-analysis.** Only 4 of 25 usable reference studies reported a confidence interval, so nothing is pooled; medians and ranges only.
- **Consequence for confidence language.** A tactic absent from the catalogue gets "no published measurement found", never "does not work". A tactic present with one sample gets "one well-designed test", never "the evidence shows". A MAINSTREAM tactic in GEO or answer formatting gets its sample count and design stated in the same breath, because the family it sits in is where the corpus is thinnest.

## Citing the study

- **Citation line (as printed in the study):** Krešimir Ćorluka, September 2026. *The SEO Meta Study: a systematic review of SEO, AEO and GEO case studies.* Edition 1. Ovisy, Zagreb. ovisy.hr — the blog write-up uses the variant *Ćorluka, K. (2026). The SEO Meta Study: a systematic review of 9,249 documents on SEO, AEO and GEO. Ovisy.*
- **Source URLs:** PDF at https://ovisy.hr/seo-meta-study.pdf (170 pages); summary at https://ovisy.hr/blog/seo-meta-study. Edition 1, September 2026. Corrections to info@ovisy.hr.
- **Keep the denominator.** The author's rule: "the number keeps its denominator, because 602 on its own means nothing without the 769". Quote "602 of 769", "92 of 182", "65 of 118", "81.9% of 1,700" — never the numerator alone. And do not equate documents (9,249), sources (552 primary; 793 in the study's source-type and recency tallies), independent samples, controlled measurements (118) and tactic clusters (1,105 → 769 → 182): they are five different counts.
- **Pipeline for context:** 30,000+ crawled → 9,249 documents → 9,177 after dedup → 9,033 after quality filter → 1,391 with a measured outcome → 7,329 findings → 1,105 tactic clusters → 769 past the evidence gates → 182 entries printed. Two blind model passes (Google + OpenAI), a verbatim-quote requirement, a deterministic number-in-source check, and 90 documents hand-verified.
- **Licence:** figures, single tables and passages may be quoted and reused with a credit, with no licence, fee or link required. Republishing the review, or a substantial part of it, in any language or under any other name needs permission (info@ovisy.hr, "usually given"). The measurements belong to the publishers named with them — cite those too, because they did the measuring. There is no verbatim quotation anywhere in the study.
- **Cite the sources, not just the study.** Every entry names its sources with the design each used; the Evidence Appendix prints the full address of every one. When a figure is load-bearing for a client, open the original.
- **The study reports its own error rate.** 78 coding errors were caught during verification (rollbacks grouped with rollouts; one A/B test appearing as two opposite cards; +12% printed for a −12% decline; vendor content over-flagged at 74% against an actual 8%). Its own words: "It can still have errors in it." Treat any single printed figure as correctable, and prefer the source when the two disagree.
