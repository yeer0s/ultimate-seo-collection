## How to read this file

Everything in this file is `[practitioner]` unless it carries a documented label (engine documentation, cited with its URL and read date) or carries a study bridge `[measured: MARK #N]` pointing at an entry in `evidence-and-tactics-1.md`, where the sample count, controlled count, and printed effect live. A mark here is a pointer, not a verdict: MAINSTREAM means published at least three times, not "works".

**Two evidence tiers live in this file, and they are not interchangeable.** Both are `[practitioner]`; what separates them is whether the attribution was ever checked.

**Secondary layer — pre-collection discourse of the general, professional and agentic-SEO practitioners** (the "Consensus canon", "Contrarian takes" and everything below them; those three communities now have primary layers, next to the technical practitioners' — the general one since 2026-10-11). A digest dated 2026-09-22 built from search-engine snippets and 12 third-party write-ups that quote or summarise threads — the original discussions were never opened and no score was observed directly. Read "scores not visible" literally. Nothing here was compared against an original thread, so a misquote inside a write-up propagates into this file undetected, and a "consensus" is the write-up author's reading of a thread rather than a counted one. Treat these claims as leads to verify, not as findings, and never attribute a specific score or wording to a named practitioner on this layer's authority alone.

**Primary layer — technical, agentic-SEO, professional and general practitioners** (the four sections immediately below). A complete credential-free collection of each community's public discussions (anonymised sources) with real scores and full comment bodies, where every cited post and comment id was checked back against the corpus. Scores are counted, not estimated; consensus labels record what the thread actually did with a claim. That buys exactly one thing: the attribution is sound — a quote, a score and a date can be relied on and re-derived. It buys nothing about correctness. A heavily-scored practice is a practice many practitioners agree with, which is why this layer is still `[practitioner]` and never `[measured]`.

The SEO Meta Study's own caveat is that its corpus cannot see LinkedIn, Slack, conference decks or NDA client work — the operating judgment that practitioners actually run on. This file is where that missing layer partly lives, and it inherits the same publication bias in a sharper form: people post wins and disasters, never nulls, and a community's loudest voice is a moderator with a house position.

Rule: a practitioner claim never carries a percentage into a client report. It is a hypothesis to test with the six-part recipe (when it applies · before you start · what to change · what to keep · what to measure · when to revert) in `evidence-and-tactics-1.md`, using the holdout, MDE and revert-trigger procedure in `experiments-and-revert-2.md`.
