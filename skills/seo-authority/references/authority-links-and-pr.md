## What the published record says about links

The link family is the study's clearest case of a tactic that is alive by count and unreadable by size. Every entry is MAINSTREAM, agency-authored, measured before/after on the agency's own client, and most bundle links with on-page edits — the number belongs to the bundle.

- **#8 Pitch publishers for editorial backlinks** — `[measured: MAINSTREAM, n=19 across 14 publishers, c=0]` median +71% organic sessions, range +13% to +366.1%, 19 of 19 same direction, 2018–2026. Every appendix design is "before/after, no control" or "single case". Recipe: pitch relevant publications with original data, expert quotes or media assets; keep on-page elements fixed.
- **#9 Guest posts with contextual backlinks** — `[measured: MAINSTREAM, n=8 across 7 publishers, c=0]` median +45% organic sessions, range +30% to +1,811%.
- **#10 Guest posts + page headers** — `[measured: MAINSTREAM, n=6, c=0]` headline +212%, median +228.1% organic sessions; the recipe also rewrote titles/H1s and internal links (40 guest links over 6 months, control set untouched). Bundle.
- **#11 Content + links + code cleanup** — `[measured: MAINSTREAM, n=26 across 20 publishers, c=0]` median +214% organic sessions, range +30% to +787%. Bundle.
- **#12 Guest backlinks + edit target pages** — `[measured: MAINSTREAM, n=7 across 3 publishers, c=0]` headline +432% (one site), median +231% organic sessions. Bundle.
- **#14 Disavow + on-page text** — `[measured: MAINSTREAM, n=14 across 10 publishers, c=0]` headline +65%, **no median — values run −12% to +497% and cross zero**; one sample (ahrefs.com 2024, 3 pages) is −7.1%. Bundle.
- **#15 Survey research reports + press pitch** — `[measured: MAINSTREAM, n=8 across 7 publishers, c=0]` median +103% organic sessions, range +100% to +200%; resource page with charts, media pitching, title edits on related pages, held-out section. Bundle.
- **#13 Disavow files for identified spam links** — `[measured: IT DEPENDS, n=3, c=0]` +80% organic sessions for sites recovering from an *active manual link penalty*; direction only for proactive disavow without one. Moderator: *is there an active manual action?* Google describes disavow for sites with a considerable number of spammy, artificial or low-quality links that have caused, or likely will cause, a manual action, once removal has been tried, and warns that incorrect use can harm performance `[documented: https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/2648487, 2026-10-11]`. Routing: skill `seo-technical` › migrations-and-recovery.
- **What stays fixed during a link cleanup and reconsideration:** content, titles, internal links and every link you are not removing, from the first removal until the review answer and one full measurement window after it, so the outcome reads against the cleanup alone `[unvalidated house heuristic]`.

Read the family against the study's denominators: 81.9% of 1,700 published outcomes are positive (1,392 improvements, 196 mixed, 65 declines, 46 no effect); a hand-checked 90 came out 64% positive (skill `seo-evidence` › evidence-and-tactics-1). The link entries sit at the far end of that distribution — zero controlled designs, and the people measuring were the people selling the service.

**Carry it as:** *editorial outreach is alive by count and publication-biased by construction.* The record supports relevant editorial outreach measured on your own site with a matched holdout; it does not support budgeting for +71%, and it does not license the next section's refusals.

## The policy line

Refused regardless of what any case study prints `[documented: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies, 2026-10-11]`: buying or selling links that pass ranking signal (including sponsored placements without `rel="sponsored"`/`nofollow`); private blog networks and expired-domain networks; cross-linking a portfolio of sites built to pass PageRank to each other; service-for-link and link-for-link swaps; guest posting at scale with keyword-rich anchors as a link scheme — #9/#10's counts do not change the policy status of the pattern once industrialised.

Fine: **one editorial link from a property you own or partner with, placed where a reader would expect it** — the parent company's "our brands" page, a partner's resource list, a blog post that genuinely covers the launch. The deciding property is **purpose and footprint, not ownership**. Corporate navigation a user would click is navigation; twenty owned domains with sitewide footer links to each other is a network. This reconciles audit point 8 in skill `seo-optimizer` ("One editorial link from an owned property a reader expects") with the refusal of portfolio cross-linking (this skill's rule 1): both hold once the test is *would this link exist for a reader?*

No exchange rate attaches to any link, and no "engagement makes Google notice" mechanism is claimed. A relevant referral link is worth having because it sends readers and is the kind of link engines document as legitimate.

## Linkable assets and digital PR

The evidence points at the asset, not the pitch: #15 is the only link entry whose recipe starts with something the site *made*, and #8's recipe requires original data or media assets before outreach.

- **Original data and surveys** `[measured: #15 MAINSTREAM, n=8, c=0, +103% organic sessions, bundle]` — for a comparison platform the dataset exists: tariff deltas by kVA band, provider price movements after an ERSE decision, IRS bracket effects by household type. State coverage (which providers, which date), selection (what was excluded and why) and how to reproduce the table `[unvalidated house heuristic]`. A report a journalist can check is one they can cite.
- **Free tools and calculators** — a correct, crawlable calculator answering a searched question earns references unpitched `[hypothesis]`. Validation rules are in the E-E-A-T section; a wrong calculator is a linkable liability.
- **Reproducible Portuguese price/fee reports** `[unvalidated house heuristic]` — periodic editions (quarterly electricity, annual bank fees) with a stable table structure so each can be compared to the last and cited as a series. Coverage and selection limits go in the body, not a footnote.
- **Reclaim earned links and unlinked mentions** `[unvalidated house heuristic]` — fix or request updates for broken inbound links to moved URLs; ask once for a link on press mentions that name the brand without one. Recovers what was earned; manufactures nothing.
- **Journalist pitching that is relevant, not volume** `[unvalidated house heuristic]` — the specific journalist who covers energy prices or personal finance, the specific finding with its chart, on the day it matters (tariff publication, Budget day). Mass outreach is #8 at its most publication-biased and least repeatable.
- **Domain consolidation + PR** — `[measured: #145 MAINSTREAM, n=5 across 4 publishers, c=0]` headline +96% (single case), median +59.5% ranking positions, range +23% to +96%. Bundle; the redirect half is a migration (skill `seo-technical` › migrations-and-recovery, #133).

### Which formats attract links — one vendor's numbers

Foundation Inc. (Mar 2026) published links-to-page share by content format, measured on B2B SaaS sites, observational, no controls, sample size not reported `[vendor]`:

| Format | Links vs page share |
|---|---|
| Stat/data roundups | 4.25× |
| Glossary | 1.47× |
| Tools/calculators | 1.38× |
| How-to | 1.36× |
| Original research | 0.80× |
| Ultimate guides | 0.77× |
| Thought leadership | 0.74× |
| Templates | 0.68× |

Read the ranking as direction only: B2B SaaS is not consumer comparison. A possible reason roundups lead is that writers link whatever is easiest to cite, so original research tends to be cited second-hand, through a roundup `[hypothesis]`. When we publish original data, ship alongside it a stat page of one-line findings, each dated and sourced, so the citable sentence lives on our domain `[unvalidated house heuristic]`.

### The information-gain gate

A named gate other files point to. Before writing any piece meant to rank, write one sentence saying what it adds that none of the current top results has. If that sentence cannot be written, the piece is not written `[unvalidated house heuristic]`.

## How authority accrues — hypotheses, labelled

Mechanism stories, two from antitrust testimony and the 2024 Content Warehouse API leak, both publicly downplayed by Google. They explain patterns; they are not operating rules, and none says how much a signal weighs.

- **Site-level quality signals** `[hypothesis]` — a host-level component (the leak's `siteAuthority`) lifts pages on a strong domain and caps pages on a weak one. Not Moz "DA", and per-page relevance still decides individual results.
- **Satisfaction signals** `[hypothesis]` — result-resolution behaviour (Navboost, "last longest click", pogo-sticking) feeds ranking. Not dwell time or bounce rate — an answer page bounces by design. Whatever the weight, click bots are spam: Google's spam policies prohibit machine-generated traffic, i.e. automated queries and other automated access to Google Search without express permission `[documented: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies, 2026-10-11]`; it is earned only by ending the searcher's task.
- **PageRank as distance from trusted seeds** `[hypothesis]` — link trust propagates from a seed set, so proximity to a trusted hub matters more than link count. Consistent with the policy line: editorial placement, not volume.

Two patterns on top:

- **The no-name-to-giant long-tail cluster play** — compete with national brands on second-tier head terms and the long tail first `[practitioner: anonymised sources · consensus · 2 thr / 39 · newest 2022]`. Our extension: fully own a tight cluster the incumbents gloss over (every kVA band, every municipality's IMI rate), earn clicks and references there, then widen the circle; cluster depth, not a hero page with a link blast, is the lever `[hypothesis]`.
- **Contextual relevance, not niche-lock** `[hypothesis]` — a descriptive-anchor link from an adjacent business (an accountancy blog to a salary calculator) is a normal editorial link; "same niche only" has no support we hold. Irrelevant links at scale are a policy problem.
